TREASURE HUNTERS · PLAY & REFERENCE

Four adventures. Two unusual dice. One explorer who would very much like to get there first.

Learn with Quick start, then use the contents to look up a rule. The adventures are independent; you do not need to finish one to play another. Story chapters in the game are optional and labelled for spoilers. This guide explains the rules without revealing the endings.

Quick start

  1. Choose Play solo to race against NPCs, or create a multiplayer lobby. Choose your name, token design and colour.
  2. Pick a map. For a first game, try The Oracle with the built-in settings and classes off.
  3. Check your goal and the map legend. Your first destination is not necessarily your final one.
  4. On your turn, draw the scheduled items, use any optional start actions, then plan your route before rolling. Choose at junctions until the route is complete.
  5. Optionally place items on eligible squares of that route. Roll the two dice and follow the indicated part of your plan. Resolve any decisions the game offers.
  6. After movement, optionally discard unwanted items, then press End turn. Race objectives determine the winner; gold and HP serve other purposes.

For multiplayer, every human reviews and accepts the lobby proposal. The host presses Start game. Changing the proposal requires everyone to accept again.

Dice, movement and reading speed

The dice reveal their result near the end of the throw. Your token then moves; encounter cards appear shortly after arrival. A brief turn cue precedes the next turn's item message. Gameplay buttons wait for this visual sequence to finish.

Under Control deck → Story & event display, Quick animations shortens throws, movement and turn cues. Quick Chance cards is separate: it skips Continue acknowledgements, but never makes a gameplay choice for you. Completed cards remain readable while play continues. Your device's reduced-motion setting shows results directly. These preferences do not change dice odds or game rules.

Unfinished solo setups and defaults

Back to menu keeps an unfinished solo setup during this visit. Changing saved defaults does not silently replace its choices. Use Reset to current defaults in solo setup to replace its map and game rules with the latest saved defaults, or Discard setup to return to the menu and start fresh next time.

Reset preserves your name, token appearances and opponent count, reducing the count only if the new map has a lower limit. It clears the selected starting home. After Save defaults, an existing solo draft offers Update solo setup or Keep setup. These controls affect only unstarted solo setup; existing games and created multiplayer lobbies keep their rules. Drafts are held in memory and are not saved games.

Every player controls one token. The host participates as a player. Solo play is one human against one or more NPCs, within the selected map's capacity. An optional shared screen is a public observer, outside those limits; it has no token, bag or turn.

The ordinary profile starts with 100 HP, 0 gold, two items, a five-item bag, one item drawn per playable turn, and one Resource action per turn. Specialist classes can change HP, income and bag capacity. Match settings can change the other listed quantities.

A turn, step by step

Start. If you are Stunned, remove one Stunned stack and miss that turn: no draws, planning or chosen actions. Otherwise receive scheduled draws and next-playable-turn effects. Terrain and classes may modify this step.

Prepare. Choose optional start actions: heal, use a Zap Laser, Magnet or Winged sandals, use an available class ability, or a map-specific action. Finish these before planning. Skipping unused actions is free.

Plan. Choose a continuous intended route before seeing the roll. Single roads fill automatically; at branches, choose your exit. The ordinary maximum is six steps. Sprinter can plan up to twelve for Overdrive; Pace has a three-step base horizon. The game offers only legal continuations. You normally cannot turn straight back within one movement, except at a physical dead end. Direction can change on a later turn.

Place. Deploy eligible items while you have cards, actions and eligible squares. A Wall may also go on your own current ordinary square. Other placement and digging must use your planned route. A short roll does not refund an unreached placement. Before rolling, select one of your placements from this turn to undo it: its original item and one action return. Digging undo removes only the dug Hole and leaves compatible overlays. Completed healing, attacks and information reveals cannot be undone.

Roll and move. Follow the rolled prefix of the committed route. You cannot freely reroute after the roll. Walls and other pending choices may interrupt resolution; choose among the game's legal options. Deployment is over once you roll.

Finish. Resolve encounters, Hole choices, goals and Chance. If items remain, there is an optional discard window. Select individual items or all, review the confirmation, and confirm or cancel. Discarding costs no action and draws no replacements. Press End turn, including after discarding the entire bag. An empty bag on arrival at this window ends automatically unless a class Shove choice remains. A race victory ends immediately.

What costs an action?

Chosen start-item uses, deployments and digging share the configured Resource action budget. The default is one; the permitted range is zero to five. One chosen use normally costs one action.

Reactive Rope/Ladder Wall bypass, grenade Wall destruction, Pickaxe mining and Shovel Hole travel consume their item but cost no Resource action. They remain available with a zero budget. Automatic Armor and Dagger use, planning, movement, voluntary discards and Chance decisions also cost no Resource action. Purchases still need their stated gold.

With specialists enabled, each playable turn also has one separate class action. A free passive uses neither allowance. Angry Men's Objection costs gold and uses the movement-modifier slot, not an action. Full-turn Wall dismantling spends the whole turn instead of an action.

Movement modifiers are declared before rolling where required. Ordinary modified movement is clamped to 1–6; Sprinter Cramp, Overdrive and Slow Motion have their own rules. The displayed dice retain their actual faces even when movement is modified.

Exact arrival is the default: enter an eligible destination only when the remaining movement exactly pays for that step. If you would overshoot it, you cannot simply stop there. Surplus arrival, when selected for the match, lets an eligible destination absorb the extra movement. Requirements such as installed seals or Angry Men qualification still apply.

An ordinary square is not an optional stopping point. Volcano's predeclared eligible worksite stops are a specific exception. Each drawn road segment is a step, including Map 4's explicit diagonal roads; visual proximity does not create a connection.

Items and the bag

Items are Resource cards. The separate Chance deck produces events and can award Resource cards. Blue/red informational Event presentations are messages, not a third deck or extra inventory.

The Resource deck contains 44 original cards. Used or discarded cards enter its discard pile and are shuffled back only when needed. If both piles are empty, no item is drawn. A full bag discards the newly drawn item; it does not offer temporary extra space or replace something already held. Starting deals must fit both the bag and the shared deck.

Walls, placement and activation

Walls, Bombs and Banana peels are marked OFF until their deployment turn actually ends, including the discard window. They cannot affect anyone during that turn. Activation under a token causes no entry effect. An active Wall underneath you gates your later exit. Traps are immediately active and permanently ignore their owner.

Placements exclude safe landmarks, Holes, existing incompatible objects and occupied squares. The current-square Wall exception ignores only your own token; another token still prevents placement. Coins and Stars can remain as compatible overlays where permitted. The game highlights legal locations.

A reached Wall offers Stop or an available tool. Rope/Ladder needs movement left and a legal exit on your plan. Bypass leaves the Wall standing and grants no movement. A Hand Grenade removes it even with zero movement left, without creating a Bomb there. Unusable tools are not consumed.

Dismantle Wall is card-free but takes the whole otherwise unused turn. It is available for an eligible Wall underfoot or on the first route square, before rolling or using an item/class action or buying Objection. It prevents a permanent lock even at zero Resource actions. Confirming it gives no movement; any subsequent discard window still needs completion.

HP, captures and conditions

HP measures survival; gold pays explicit costs and qualifies Angry Men finishes. There is no player Score balance. Completing a map objective wins the race.

At zero HP or capture, return to your own home and restore class maximum HP. Stunned and Brace clear. Keep bag items, gold except explicit theft, and permanent map progress: Oracle destinations, Angry Men coverage/stage subject to its gold-loss rule, or installed Volcano seals. A carried uninstalled Volcano seal is lost. Pending next-playable effects and Clear Head survive resets. A reset cancels the interrupted movement.

Captures, when enabled, happen on final landing on an opponent outside safe landmarks; merely passing does not capture. Resolve opponents in stable seat order, stopping if the incoming player is reset. Safe landmarks permit stacking and protect from capture; Angry Men private homes are restricted to their owner. Turning captures off allows ordinary sharing and disables Dagger combat and capture theft, while hazards and other effects remain.

An attacking Dagger is automatically consumed even against an unarmed defender. A Stunned defender cannot use their Dagger and keeps it; Engineer cannot auto-use Daggers. Defensive outcomes use the frozen match setting:

Response Defensive outcome
Parry Defending Dagger cancels capture and theft; both stay. Two Daggers: attacker captures.
Knockback Defending Dagger cancels capture and retreats the attacker up to two traveled squares. Two Daggers: attacker captures.
Duel Defender wins 60% with the only Dagger; attacker wins 60% with two Daggers. Winner captures; participating Daggers are consumed.
Parting cut Attacker captures; defending Dagger deals 40 base damage afterward. Armor blocks the cut. Two Daggers: same outcome.
Counter-capture Defending Dagger captures an unarmed attacker. Two Daggers: attacker captures, then takes a 40-base-damage cut; Armor protects.

Armor can prevent a Dagger parting cut, not capture. Without eligible Daggers, declared Barbarian Brace can reverse one incoming unarmed capture. Angry Men capture steals up to 25 gold.

Board reference

Coins: collect on successful entry, even when passing, for 10 gold; Banker gets 20. Collection removes that Coin. Spawning under a token is not entry. A later round may spawn another Coin there. Active Wall entry must be resolved first.

Banker — Procure: before planning, spend one class action and 20 gold to retrieve an eligible Resource card from the public discard. Each physical card can be procured once until the Resource discard is reshuffled. Used or discarded purchases still count in the ordinary discard pile and recycle normally. If no eligible discarded cards remain, Procure is unavailable; it cannot purchase a random draw. A normal Resource-discard reshuffle resets eligibility for all cards. Normal bag capacity and overflow apply.

Black Holes: on a fresh qualifying landing choose Pickaxe mining, Shovel travel, or one Stunned stack. Pickaxe gives 20 gold, or 40 for Banker. Shovel travels to a randomly selected different Hole, ends the movement and can resolve destination Coin collection/capture; it does not chain Hole interactions, grant destination progress or trigger another Star. Passing, remaining there, a reset or teleport arrival does not repeatedly stun you. Resolve the Hole before discarding. At most one tool interaction per turn.

Chance Stars: persist after use. A normal landing draws one Chance, at most once per token/square/turn. Passing, resets, teleport arrivals and Chance Run do not trigger them. Hole and Star can share a square after digging: resolve the Hole first; travel suppresses the original Star. Victory ends play before another Chance.

Traps/Pocket Traps: affect enemy landing, not passing; owners are immune. A normal Trap deals 50 HP; a Pocket Trap 25. Triggered Traps are removed, including when Armor protects.

Bombs: once active, entry deals 25 HP, including to the owner. Radius zero is the default. A selected radius of 1–3 open-road steps additionally deals 15 HP to other players in range; safe landmarks protect those collateral victims. Walls do not stop the blast, closed roads do. There are no chain explosions.

Banana peels: once active, entry removes up to two movement still remaining, then deals 10 HP for each point it could not remove. Landing with none left deals 20 HP. Armor does not protect. Owners are affected.

Ninjas: a deployed Ninja activates after its deployment turn and challenges the next entrant, including its recruiter. Roll the usual dice: 4–6 wins; 1–3 loses 25 HP, unless Armor protects. The encountered Ninja then leaves. Recruitment is offered only by the relevant Chance, not by woodland ambushes or an existing board Ninja.

Roadsigns: point along one fixed adjacent exit. Follow that exit only when it is otherwise legal. A sign never creates a road or grants movement.

Initial Holes and Stars use randomized road-distance spacing rather than fixed locations. Each begins at 0–8, defaults four and five, on distinct ordinary squares excluding safe landmarks. Digging can later increase Holes to twelve. Saved games retain their exact layout.

Map 1: The Oracle

Start at shared S. Reach a first shrine at A, B or C, or the specific first shrine chosen in settings. That visit records your first destination and assigns a different second shrine. Be first to reach your assigned second shrine to win. Both visits follow the chosen arrival rule.

The map has 57 squares on a tall, branching layout. The lower loops offer alternative approaches; the upper junction connects the three shrine routes. Each visible connection is one step. You choose branches before rolling, rather than adding the dice to a linear square number.

Returning home after a setback does not erase the first shrine or assigned target. S and the shrines are safe landmarks. The game's first-shrine presentation explains the assignment when you discover it; the story adds no bag item or gold award.

Map 2: Volcano Clock

The 187-square map has an outer ring of storage cabinets U, middle valve junctions J, inner cooling stations C, six outer refuges S, and central summit V. Roads join rings through primary spokes and secondary crossings. Ring roads stay open.

Collect a numbered containment seal at Uj, carry one at a time, and install it at the opposite C station: U1→C4, U2→C5, U3→C6, and the reverse pairs. Installed seals are permanent. Supplies do not deny other players a seal. A capture, zero HP or Hole travel loses only the carried seal.

  • A — Seal Relay: install all six distinct seals, then reach V.
  • B — Valve Wars: install any five distinct seals, then reach V. At a J valve you may spend 20 gold and no Resource action to close both secondary crossings in its sector for the next two rounds. Orders affect everyone; an owner/sector cannot have overlapping orders.

During planning, choose Stop and activate at an eligible worksite to brake there and interact if the roll reaches it. Choose Pass to continue. Falling short does nothing. Forced arrivals do not activate worksites. Summit entry still requires qualification and the selected destination-arrival rule.

Watch the forecast. A rotating closure advances every configured 1–3 rounds. On an actual rotation, exposed tokens in the sector being swept take the selected Lava sweep damage: 35 HP by default, adjustable from 0–100 HP in Map 2 settings. The sweep covers every square in the red wedge, including both boundary spokes and the middle spoke. A boundary spoke is exposed to sweeps from either adjacent sector. Banker takes double damage. Zero disables sweep damage while closures and rotation continue. Only refuges and V shelter you; U/C worksites permit stacking but are exposed. Armor does not protect against lava. Closure width changes the closed radial gates, independently of sweep damage. Ring roads never close, and the game shows current and upcoming sweeps before you plan. The separate 5-HP magma-terrain damage is unchanged.

Map 3: Angry Men

The board has 40 circuit squares and four private homes. Each home joins the circuit at its owner's entrance. Two-player games use opposite homes. Passing a track square counts toward coverage; teleport/reset/initial placement does not. Coverage survives setbacks.

  1. Earning circuit: visit all forty different track squares, then return voluntarily to your entrance. Passing the entrance is enough: bank 100 gold, clear coverage to the entrance and begin another circuit. Remaining movement can continue. Standing there never pays repeatedly.
  2. Qualify: after banking, if your held gold meets the match's requirement, the next circuit becomes a final attempt. The default requirement is 250, adjustable from 150 to 500. Reaching the requirement halfway through an earning circuit does not promote that circuit.
  3. Final attempt: cover the circuit again and return to your entrance. This completion awards no automatic 100 gold. Then enter your own home with enough gold, using exact or surplus arrival as configured. Remaining movement can finish in the same roll if legal.

There is no mandatory minimum of two earning circuits. Example: at the default 250, collecting enough outside income to hold 150 before banking earns 100 and qualifies the next circuit. Gold is held, not paid, when you finish.

Banking shows Circuit banked! if another earning circuit begins, or The home stretch! if a potentially final circuit begins. Both cards show the 100-gold reward and new balance. Completing the final circuit is recorded in the Island Journal and circuit status, without another milestone card before the ending. Gold-loss rules can still affect the attempt.

Gold-loss options:

  • Allow win with enough gold on finish (default): keep final coverage/readiness after a loss, but restore the required gold before entering home. At turn start on your entrance, a completed low-gold final attempt can instead be banked for 100 gold and restarted. This trades away the ready finish; you must complete another circuit.
  • Another circuit required: falling from enough gold to below the requirement cancels a final/ready attempt into an earning circuit, retaining visited squares. Recovering gold alone does not restore final status. Finish that earning circuit, bank, qualify again and cover a new final circuit.

Example: with requirement 250, falling from 270 to 240 during a strict final attempt cancels it. Even if you recover to 280 immediately, finish/bank that earning circuit before starting a fresh final one.

Objection! Pay 20 gold before planning to declare +1 or −1 movement. It shares the once-per-turn modifier with Winged sandals; you cannot use both. It costs no Resource action and can itself invalidate a strict final attempt. Captures steal up to 25 gold.

The setup recommendation for new Coins per round is ceil(players / 2). It is advice, not an automatic change or guaranteed income. Players compete for the same Coins.

Map 4: X Marks the Core

Be first to reach central C under the chosen arrival rule. No seal or gold qualification is required. Eight perimeter homes are drawn, but the playable capacity is four players; authored homes are not extra seats.

The 393-square map has an outer square, middle octagon, inner square and central cross. The outer quadrants are mountain (northwest), sand (northeast), magma (southwest) and wood (southeast); the inner quadrants are wood, magma, sand and mountain in those same positions. Every explicit segment, including diagonal roads, costs one movement. Outer routes allow interception and escape; four approaches lead into the centre. All homes are 59 road steps from C before hazards. Homes and C are safe; approach squares are ordinary.

Specialist classes

Classes are off by default: everyone is an ordinary Adventurer. With Classes v1, six distinct specialists are shuffled using the setup seed and assigned without replacement. Players do not draft classes or choose custom loadouts. Token shape/colour is independent of class.

Engineer's extra bag space is capped at twenty.

Chance reference

The default deck has 50 cards: two copies of each of nineteen special events, plus twelve ordinary item draws. Deck size may be 40–100, retaining two of every special event and changing the ordinary item-draw count. After the whole deck resolves, shuffle its separate discard. A saved pending card resumes its existing choices/results; it does not redraw.

Everyone sees the Chance story, effect and public result. Only the active player chooses or continues it. Awarded items and held-item choices stay private; a committed discard is public. Quick Chance cards can advance non-choice acknowledgements but never buys something or chooses a route/item for you.

Anyone makes mistakes. The important thing is to correct them and learn from them. A small inscription on a nearby stone reads: ‘Try again.’ Beneath it, in smaller letters: ‘Once.’

Effect: You and a random opponent roll the usual dice. The higher total takes up to 30 gold from the loser. You win ties.

Effect: You may halve every opponent’s movement on their next playable turn, rounded up. Does not stack.

Cotinus coggygria! 2 cards

A beautiful shrub – and, according to a field guide written by the island’s extremely confident herbalist, exactly what those scratches needed. The page is well-thumbed. Under “Possible side effects,” someone has drawn a smiling face.

Lost Property Office 2 cards

A small unattended booth contains an assortment of things people have lost, abandoned, or thrown away with considerable force. Something here has your name on it. Probably. The claim form asks for a convincing description. “That one” seems worth a try.

A brass donation box offers a blessing in exchange for a modest contribution. The inscription promises ‘a second opportunity when circumstances prove disappointing.’ Below it, in extremely small letters: ‘No third opportunities. No cash refunds. Blessing may be non-transferable.’

Have You Tried Turning It Off and On Again? 2 cards

A roadside diagnostic unit scans you from boots to hat. ‘Accumulated faults detected.’ Before you can object to the terminology, it asks you to close your eyes, take a breath, and reconsider your recent decisions. The screen briefly goes black. You find this surprisingly helpful.

Effect: Pay 15 gold to restore your HP to its maximum, or decline. Stunned and other conditions are unchanged.

Unattended Baggage 2 cards

A perfectly serviceable item lies beside a sign reading ‘Do not leave baggage unattended.’ You look left. You look right. A loudspeaker clears its throat. You decide to comply with the sign by attending to the item personally.

Effect: Draw 2 items, then discard 1 item of your choice from your bag. Normal bag limits apply to each draw. If your bag is empty afterward, there is nothing to discard.

Multiplayer and recovery

  1. Open the same reachable HTTPS game address on all devices. A laptop's localhost address is not reachable as localhost from a phone. The phone must trust the certificate.
  2. The host creates a lobby, adds human/NPC seats, and chooses Invite / reconnect player for each human. Each guest needs their own invitation.
  3. Scan the invitation QR, or paste the invitation link/text into the guest connection screen and create an answer. Send that answer to the host. The host scans/pastes it in the original host tab and presses Connect.
  4. After sharing the answer, the guest may need Start connection after sending answer. Keep the same original tab. Once connected, choose your own name/token, review and accept the proposal. The host starts once all required humans have accepted.

Both devices can use the same server port. The long code contains connection paths, credentials and seat binding; it is not a per-turn exchange. Keep invitations and answers private. Text and local QR-image scanning remain available if a camera cannot be used. A multipart QR uses multiple frames; follow the scanner's instructions rather than copying one frame as a complete code.

If a human disconnects, play pauses. Reconnect that seat without replacing healthy guests, wait until all humans are ready, then the host presses Resume. Recovery does not automatically resume play. If the host page actually restarts, the original host restores its private checkpoint and every human needs fresh signaling. There is no automatic replacement by an NPC or host migration.

Brief app switches are handled best effort, but iPhone/iPad may suspend or discard a page. Keep the host foreground for active play. Installing as a Home Screen app does not guarantee background execution, offline access or cross-network reachability. Different networks may require a configured TURN relay; none is provisioned by the game. More sharing time in the interface cannot override native connection failure.

The in-app guide stays in the current window, including when used as a Home Screen web app. Close it to return to the existing screen; it does not reload the game or automatically unpause it. The standalone guide also works as one HTML document with a same-tab Main Menu link when served with the game. Downloaded copies remain readable offline, but their Main Menu link requires the game address to be reachable.

Saves, results and quick reference

The trusted host checkpoints committed changes. Use the device menu to export a backup before replacing a game; exporting alone does not approve replacement. Restore the host's private save to resume authority. A guest's cached display is not a host backup. Failed imports should leave the current checkpoint intact. Only the current save format is accepted; old games are not migrated.

Completed results record map settings, standings and statistics. Reaching the round limit records rankings without a race winner. Ranking follows map progress and relevant remaining distance, not a Score balance; ties can share rank. Local lifetime statistics and their backup/restore controls are separate from a running game save.

Useful distinctions: landing is the final arrival after movement; passing enters a square without ending there. Entry hazards can affect passing while landing Traps/captures do not. Teleport and reset are separate arrival types. A round completes after all seats have taken their scheduled turns, including skipped ones. A playable turn is one not skipped by Stunned.

Where to look in the game: Actions contains required decisions; Bag shows held items; Status shows the active goal and match facts; Players shows public progress/classes; Journal and Recent cards preserve recent results. OFF marks an inactive deployment. Player symbols and names accompany colours.

Milestones, endings and finished games

Important milestones give their achiever the full story and next-step instructions. Other explorers receive a shorter blue announcement; observers receive a factual public version. Reviewing an older card shows the facts recorded at that milestone. Destinations and progress that were already public remain public.

After the final movement and arrival pause, an ending card announces your victory, defeat, or a round-limit result with no winner. It contains your expedition ending; losing players do not also receive the winner's ending. Dismiss it without losing the story: Expedition ending remains collapsed in Actions for rereading. The longer story follows your local story-display preference. Observers see the winner's public ending. Full chapters remain available under The story so far.

Close game and return to menu closes a completed game. Abandon applies to unfinished games. Before closing or replacing a finished checkpoint, the app preserves its statistics and detailed archive. If preservation fails, the checkpoint stays until you keep it, export it, or explicitly continue without preservation. Closing on one device does not delete another player's saved copy.

Under Ship ledger → Recent results, the two most recent completed games have read-only View finished game and export controls. Archives retain the board, up to 1,000 journal events, and up to 30 recent cards; they are not complete replays. Archiving the next game replaces the oldest detailed archive. Its result statistics remain. Host exports are private encrypted game files; guest/observer exports are filtered read-only JSON records without reconnect credentials. Statistics exports do not include detailed archives. Export a game separately to keep it indefinitely. Archives are local to this browser/device and do not create another active session.
